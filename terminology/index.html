Terminology & structure reviewTask 3.1 workflow against ISO 19650 · t3 of 2026-11-12

What this is

Nine findings on where the task 3.1 workflow’s wording and structure should change, and where it should deliberately not. Each says what we write now, what the source says, what I recommend, and what it would cost.

Source. F1–F9 come from BIM Corner, ISO 19650 terms explained in this simple way (27 Jan 2021), plus our own workflow data. The article states it is not comprehensive and that its author is not ISO-certified, so every claim in those nine is attributed to it rather than to the standard, and anything it does not cover is marked as such.

F10–F15 are different. They come from the UK BIM Framework, Information management according to BS EN ISO 19650, Guidance Part 2: Processes for Project Delivery, edition 3, January 2020, 135 pp., published by bsi, cdbb and the UK BIM Alliance, together with the Design–Bid–Build process map the partners sent us. Where one of those findings states what the standard requires, it is read from that document, with the clause number given. F8 asked us to leave container states alone “until someone has read the standard rather than a blog” — F12 is that reading.

Answer each one, write what you think, and export at the bottom. Send the file back and the next build shows your answers next to each other.

F1

Two vocabularies are being mixed in one field

wording

What we write now

Stakeholders read Building owners (SG4) appointing party → architects and engineers (SG1) in version2 of HLUC1-A1-E1. SG1–SG14 name sectors: architects and engineers, building owners, contractors, facility operators.

What the source says

BIM Corner defines the ISO 19650 roles as positions in one appointment: the appointing party is “a client, building owner or an end-user – owner of the project”; the lead appointed party “is assigned directly by the client, and takes the role of a coordinator”; appointed parties are “designers, contractors, suppliers, vendors”; a task team is “the team within the Delivery team … responsible for performing a specific task”.

Recommendation

Keep the SG code as the stable identity and put the ISO role on the exchange, not on the group name. The two answer different questions: SG says who an organisation is, the ISO role says what position it holds in this appointment, and the same organisation changes role between phases.

What it costs

Writing “SG4 appointing party” everywhere would be wrong wherever SG4 is not the appointing party — in HLUC3 the facility operator often is, and in HLUC2 a contractor is frequently the lead appointed party.

F2

“High-level use case” is not a standard term

wording

What we write now

We say high-level use case and identify everything as HLUC1-A5-E3. version2 changes the prose to “use case” and “UC2”, and renamed the folders to Use_case_1.

What the source says

Neither term appears in the BIM Corner article; it is not ISO 19650 vocabulary either way. “Use case” is IDM / buildingSMART language, which is where T3.2 is heading when it maps the workflow as an IDM.

Recommendation

The rename is defensible and consistent with T3.2. Decide it once, now, and apply it everywhere rather than drifting.

What it costs

Identifiers are the catch. Every answer a partner returns is keyed on the exact string HLUC1-A5-E3. Renaming the IDs invalidates returned files, which is exactly the case where the answers version has to be bumped and older files are dropped on load. Renaming only the prose costs nothing.
F3

“Records”, “documents” and “files” should be information containers

wording

What we write now

Exchanges say “provide available asset and maintenance records”, “supplies earlier handover models and documents”, “a short project brief that records…”. Three different words for the same kind of thing.

What the source says

BIM Corner: an information container is “any form of unique file”, structured (geometric models, schedules) or unstructured (PDFs, scans, photos), and each “should have a unique ID and metadata such as status, revision and classification”.

Recommendation

Use information container where we mean the thing being handed over, and keep the plain word where we mean the content. This is the term T3.2 needs anyway to produce IDS and to classify with bSDD.

What it costs

Low. It is a vocabulary pass over the exchange texts, not a structural change.
F4

What the receiver needs is an exchange information requirement

wording

What we write now

Activities say what information is needed in prose, under Purpose and in the exchange text. No term is attached to it.

What the source says

BIM Corner lists Exchange Information Requirements as the ISO 19650 replacement for PAS 1192’s Employer’s Information Requirements, and describes the EIR as the document in which the appointing party sets requirements for the parties it appoints.

Recommendation

Say explicitly that each confirmed exchange becomes an EIR line in T3.2. It makes the reviewer’s job concrete: they are not commenting on prose, they are approving a future requirement.

What it costs

None to the workflow; it is a sentence on the context page and a column heading in T3.2.
F5

Level of information need, never LOD

wording

What we write now

T3.4 is described with LOIN, but LOD appears in the project description and in tool summaries.

What the source says

BIM Corner’s PAS–to–ISO table maps “Level of Definition (aggregate of Level of Detail and Level of Information)” to Level of Information Need.

Recommendation

Use LOIN throughout and mention LOD only when quoting a tool owner who used it.

What it costs

None.
F6

HLUC1–2 build a PIM; HLUC3 operates an AIM

structure

What we write now

The handover in HLUC2 is described as handing over building information. HLUC3 then uses it. The two models are never named.

What the source says

BIM Corner: the PIM “provides all the data required to carry out the delivery phase” and lives in the project CDE; the AIM “provides all the data required to carry out the operation phase”; at handover some PIM content moves into the AIM and the rest is archived.

Recommendation

Name the HLUC2 handover as the PIM → AIM transfer. It is the single change that would anchor our structure in the standard, and it tells a reviewer exactly what that activity is for.

What it costs

Needs agreement that our CDE-s / CDE-d split lines up with PIM / AIM. It may not, and that is worth knowing before T3.3.
F7

Our unit of review is not an ISO 19650 unit

structure

What we write now

We review information exchanges inside activities inside use cases.

What the source says

The BIM Corner article does not define how an information exchange is named or delimited in ISO 19650, and does not cover the CDE states (work in progress, shared, published, archived). It names the CDE only in passing.

Recommendation

Do not claim our exchange unit is ISO 19650 terminology. It comes from IDM. Say so plainly in the context page — the alignment we are making is with IDM for the structure and with ISO 19650 for the vocabulary.

What it costs

Claiming more alignment than we have is the kind of thing a reviewer from UCL or CYPE will catch immediately.
F8

Exchanges carry no appointment direction and no container state

structure

What we write now

Each exchange has a sender, a receiver, a purpose and a tool. Nothing says whether the information is flowing up or down the appointment chain, or what state it is in.

What the source says

BIM Corner describes the chain — appointing party, lead appointed party, appointed parties, task teams — but the article does not cover container states, so that half would have to come from ISO 19650-2 itself.

Recommendation

Consider one extra field per exchange: the direction in the appointment chain. Leave container state alone until someone has read the standard rather than a blog.

What it costs

Adding a field now means every partner reviews an incomplete row. Better as a T3.2 enrichment than a change to this review.
F9

“BIM specialists integrators”

wording

What we write now

version2 of HLUC1-A8-E2 reads “IT providers and BIM specialists integrators (SG11)”.

What the source says

—

Recommendation

Reads like an edit caught mid-thought. Decide between “BIM specialists” and “BIM integrators”; SG11’s group name is “IT providers and BIM integrators”.

What it costs

If the group name changes, it changes in all 115 exchanges, not in one.
F10

Four of the eight delivery steps are missing entirely

structure

What we write now

Our 27 activities start at “agree the renovation goals and what to assess” with everyone already in the room. Nothing in the workflow procures anybody, and no exchange issues a requirement that another party is obliged to meet.

What the source says

ISO 19650-2 clause 5 runs eight steps, once per appointment: 5.1 assessment and need, 5.2 invitation to tender, 5.3 tender response, 5.4 appointment, 5.5 mobilization, 5.6 collaborative production of information, 5.7 information model delivery, 5.8 project close-out. We cover 5.1, 5.6 and 5.7. The partners’ map shows a TENDER and an APPOINTMENT gate twice over, at stage 1 and again at stage 4.

Recommendation

Add the appointment boundary to the workflow, even as a single activity per use case. 5.2 is where the appointing party issues its exchange information requirements and 5.4 is where the detailed responsibility matrix and the master information delivery plan are agreed. Those three decide what everybody downstream owes, and we currently show the owing without the deciding.

What it costs

This is the one finding that changes the shape of the workflow and not its wording, so it is also the one that costs the most. An alternative is to say explicitly in D3.1 that the workflow describes information production and assumes the appointments are already in place — defensible, but it has to be written down, because a reader who knows ISO 19650 will otherwise read the gap as an oversight.
F11

HLUC4 sits outside both parts of the standard

structure

What we write now

HLUC4 Plan deconstruction and material recovery has 7 activities and 30 exchanges, written in the same form as the other three.

What the source says

ISO 19650-2 covers the delivery phase and ISO 19650-3 the operational phase. Neither covers planning a deconstruction, or the information needed to recover materials. There is no stage, no step and no container in the standard for any of it.

Recommendation

Say so, in D3.1, as a positive claim: for HLUC4 we are extending ISO 19650 rather than applying it. That is a contribution, not a gap, and it is the part of the work the standard cannot check us on.

What it costs

If we leave it implicit, a reviewer aligning the deliverable to the standard will either mark HLUC4 non-conformant or quietly force it into 19650-3 language that does not fit. Claiming the extension openly is both more honest and safer.
F12

Information container states — there are four, and “client shared” is not one

wording

What we write now

No exchange in the workflow says what state the information is in. The partners’ map shows three boxes: PUBLISHED, CLIENT SHARED and SHARED.

What the source says

ISO 19650-1 figure 10, reproduced as figure 3 of the guidance, gives four states and only four. Work in progress: being developed by its originator or task team, not visible to anyone else. Shared: approved for sharing with other appropriate task teams and delivery teams, or with the appointing party. Published: authorized for use in more detailed design, for construction or for asset management. Archive: the journal of transactions, the audit trail.

Recommendation

Drop client shared as a state. The definition of shared already covers sharing with the appointing party, so the map’s CLIENT SHARED box is a shared container with a particular audience — keep it as a label if it is useful, but not as a fifth state. Then consider carrying the state on each exchange: it is what turns “they send it” into something auditable.

What it costs

Adding a state to all 115 exchanges is a data change and would mean partners review a row that gained a column mid-review. F8 already recommended holding this for T3.2. The naming correction costs nothing and can be made now; the new field should wait.
F13

We produce almost none of the resources the standard asks for

structure

What we write now

The workflow names a BIM model, records, drawings, specifications and a handover package. It names no requirement set, no plan and no matrix.

What the source says

The guidance lists what each step generates: the project’s information requirements, information standard, information production methods and procedures, reference information and shared resources, information protocol and common data environment (all 5.1); the exchange information requirements and tender evaluation criteria (5.2); the pre-appointment BEP, capability and capacity summary, mobilization plan and risk register (5.3); the BEP, detailed responsibility matrix, master information delivery plan and task information delivery plans (5.4).

Recommendation

Two of these we already have under other names and should simply rename: HLUC1-A1 is project information requirements work, and the ECM catalogue is the project’s reference information and shared resources — which the partners’ own drawing already labels as ISO 19650-2 clause 5.1.6. The rest follow from F10: they are produced at steps we do not have.

What it costs

Renaming what we already do is cheap and makes the workflow legible to anybody working to the standard. Inventing the rest to look conformant would be worse than not having them, because we would be describing documents nobody in the use cases ever writes.
F14

The stage names are RIBA, not ISO 19650

wording

What we write now

The partners’ map runs across STAGE 0 Strategic Definition, STAGE 1 Preparation and Briefing, STAGE 2 Concept Design, STAGE 3 Spatial Coordination, STAGE 4 Technical Design. We have adopted that row in the new overview so the two can be compared.

What the source says

Those are the RIBA Plan of Work 2020 stages, a UK convention. ISO 19650-2 does not name project stages at all: it runs its eight steps once per appointment, and a project with three appointments runs them three times.

Recommendation

Keep the stage row — it is how the partners think and it makes the map readable — but label it as RIBA Plan of Work 2020 wherever it appears, and never call a stage an ISO 19650 stage. For a project in Spain, Poland or Greece the RIBA stages are not the local ones, which is worth a sentence in D3.1.

What it costs

Low. It is a labelling discipline, not a change. Getting it wrong would have us claiming conformance to a stage framework the standard does not contain, in a deliverable that several partners will read as normative.
F15

Require, communicate, check — a free structure for the tools column

wording

What we write now

Our relevant tools column names tool codes and says what each contributes, in prose, with no classification.

What the source says

The partners’ map groups the openBIM tools into buildingSMART’s three: Require (UCM, IDS, bSDD) — state what information is needed, in a form a machine can read; Communicate (BCF) — carry issues and comments between tools; Check (IDS, validation) — test what was delivered against what was required.

Recommendation

Tag each tool entry with one of the three. It costs one word and it says what the tool is doing at that point in the workflow, which is exactly what T3.3 to T3.5 need when they pick up the tool linkages.

What it costs

Almost none, and it is reversible. The only judgement call is where a tool does two of the three — IDS is both require and check, which is the point of IDS, so it should be allowed to carry both tags rather than being forced into one.
